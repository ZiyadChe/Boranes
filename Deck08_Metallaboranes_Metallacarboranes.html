Learning outcomes

By the end of this deck you should be able to

Part A.
Metals bound through hydrogen

The M–H–B bridge: a 3c–2e bond again

  • BH4⁻ can bind a metal through one, two or three hydrogens (η1, η2, η3). Each M–H–B link is a three-centre two-electron bond, as in B2H6.
  • (Ph3P)2Cu(BH4): an early example with B–H–Cu bridges (Davidson, as reported by Hawthorne).
  • Zr(BH4)4 is volatile and molecular: each BH4 uses three bridges, so Zr is 12-coordinate by H.
  • The larger B3H8⁻ ligand binds similarly, e.g. (CO)4Mn(B3H8), counted as arachno like B4H10.
Hawthorne (1967), §5.7A, p. 312; Das, §10.12.11 ((B3H8)Mn(CO)4 as arachno); Zr(BH4)4: Greenwood & Earnshaw, 2nd edn, §6.3.3.
The drawings show the three coordination modes schematically. Zr(BH4)4 and similar tetrahydroborates (Hf, U) are among the most volatile compounds of these metals, and were studied during the Manhattan Project era as volatile uranium compounds (U(BH4)4).
Metals bound through hydrogen

Metal carbonyl anions bind BH3

  • Carbonylmetallate anions are bases. They give M–B bonded adducts: [(CO)5Re–BH3]⁻, [(CO)5Mn–BH3]⁻ (Parshall).
  • [(CO)5Re–BH3]⁻ accepts a second BH3.
  • Mn2(CO)10 + NaBH4, then H3PO4: HMn3(CO)10(BH3)2, with both Mn–H–Mn and Mn–H–B bridges, "a new structural feature" in 1967.
Hawthorne (1967), §5.7A, pp. 311–312, Fig. 5.34 (Parshall, ref. 315; ref. 317).
These were the first steps from borane chemistry towards metal clusters. The B2H5 unit in the Mn compound behaves as a diborane fragment bridging three metals.

The rule

An (18 − k)-electron metal fragment is isolobal with an (8 − k)-electron main-group fragment. The difference of 10 reflects the five extra d orbitals.

Skeletal electrons: v + x − 12 for a metal (Deck 6). Fe(CO)3: 8 + 6 − 12 = 2; CpCo: 9 + 5 − 12 = 2; both like BH.

Das, §10.12.11 (isolobal list); Mingos (2021), Table 2; R. Hoffmann, Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. Engl. 1982, 21, 711.

Metals in the cage

From B5H9 to metallaboranes

  • 1-[Fe(CO)3]B4H8: Fe(CO)3 takes the apical position of the square pyramid.
  • Count: 4 BH (8) + 4 μ-H (4) + Fe(CO)3 (2) = 14 e = 7 pairs, n = 5 → nido, as for B5H9.
  • CpCo gives two isomers, apical (1-) and basal (2-), both nido.
  • Further replacement gives [Fe(CO)3]2B3H7: the cage keeps its shape as metals replace borons.
Das, §10.12.11 and Fig. 10.12.11.1; Das, §10.13.2(f).
The drawing on the right of each pair shows the bonding: Fe or Co bonds to four basal borons and to nothing else in the cage, exactly as the apical BH does in B5H9.

Did you know?

Ferrocene can be counted as two nido cages

  • Each CpFe half is a pentagonal pyramid: five CH vertices and Fe at the apex, a nido shape derived from a pentagonal bipyramid.
  • Ferrocene is two such pyramids sharing the Fe vertex. Jemmis's mno rule: m = 2, n = 11, o = 1, p = 2 → 16 pairs.
  • Direct count: 10 CH × 3 + Fe 2 = 32 e = 16 pairs ✓. The 18-electron rule and the cluster rules agree.
Das, §10.12.10 (mno for ferrocene); Jemmis et al., J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2001, 123, 4313; Mingos (2021), Fig. 14.
This shows how far the cluster view extends: a sandwich compound and a borane cage obey the same counting.

Metallacarboranes

The dicarbollide ligand [C2B9H11]²⁻

  • From ortho-carborane: remove B3 (Deck 7), then deprotonate: nido-7,8-C2B9H11²⁻. Meta-carborane gives the 7,9 isomer.
  • The open pentagonal face has five inward-pointing orbitals holding six electrons, the same frontier set as C5H5⁻.
  • Name: Spanish olla, a pot, after its shape.

One difference from Cp⁻

The dicarbollide face orbitals point towards the missing vertex, so they overlap more strongly with a metal placed there. It binds as an η5 six-electron donor with a 2− charge.

Das, §10.13.3, Fig. 10.13.3.2; Hawthorne (1967), §5.6B.7 and §5.7B, Fig. 5.32. The labels (c) and (d) in Das's caption are interchanged in the original; the captions here follow the drawings.
Note on the figure: Das's printed labels under (c) and (d) do not match the drawings (the iron complex is the bis-ligand sandwich; the rhenium complex has one dicarbollide and three CO). The caption here describes the drawings.
Metallacarboranes · 1965

The carborane analogue of ferrocene

2 C2B9H11²⁻ + FeCl2 → [Fe(η5-C2B9H11)2]²⁻ + 2 Cl⁻

compare 2 C5H5⁻ + FeCl2 → Fe(C5H5)2

  • Fe(II), d⁶ + 2 × 6 = 18 electrons, as in ferrocene.
  • Reversibly oxidised to the Fe(III) monoanion, as ferrocene goes to ferrocenium.
  • Mixed sandwich: [CpFe(C2B9H11)]⁻, oxidised to paramagnetic CpFe(C2B9H11) (X-ray confirmed).
  • The metal completes the icosahedron of each ligand.

Metallacarboranes · counting

Two ways to count a metallacarborane

As a metal complex

  • Each dicarbollide: 6-electron donor, 2− charge.
  • [Fe(C2B9H11)2]²⁻: Fe²⁺ (d⁶) + 12 = 18 e.
  • [Co(C2B9H11)2]⁻: Co³⁺ (d⁶) + 12 = 18 e, very stable.

As a cluster

  • Half-sandwich [(C2B9H11)Re(CO)3]⁻: Re(CO)3 7 + 6 − 12 = 1; + 6 (CH) + 18 (BH) + 1 = 26 e = 13 pairs, n = 12 → closo ✓
  • Full sandwich, mno: m = 2, n = 23, o = 1 → 26 pairs ✓ (Deck 6).

Both counts agree because 18 electrons at a metal vertex and n + 1 pairs in a cage both mean "fill all bonding orbitals and none of the antibonding ones".

Das, §10.12.10 and §10.13.3; Mingos (2021), pp. 20–22, 36.
Hawthorne's group merged the 18-electron rule with the (2n + 2) skeletal electron requirement for closo cages (Jones, Evans and Hawthorne), as Mingos records.
Metallacarboranes

Half-sandwiches, and sandwiches that slip

C2B9H11²⁻ + BrM(CO)5 → [(η5-C2B9H11)M(CO)3]⁻ + Br⁻ + 2 CO

analogue of CpMn(CO)3; Re structure by X-ray

  • When the metal would exceed 18 electrons (e.g. d⁸, d⁹ ions such as Ni(II), Cu(III)/Cu(II), Au(III)), the metal slides towards the three borons of the face: a slipped sandwich.
  • Slipping weakens bonds to the two carbons and removes electrons from M–L antibonding levels, as in η5 → η3 ring slippage of Cp complexes.
Hawthorne (1967), §5.7B, p. 314, Fig. 5.36; slipped structures above 18 e: Mingos (2021), p. 21 (Hawthorne, ref. 127). Specific metal ions: recalled examples.
The slip-distortion idea was later analysed by Mingos with extended Hückel calculations. Check the specific metal examples before quoting them in an exam.
Metallacarboranes

Smaller cages, other faces

  • The same principle works for smaller nido carboranes: B8C2H10²⁻, B7C2H9²⁻, B6C2H8²⁻ each leave one vertex for a metal.
  • Other isoelectronic ligands give isomorphous sandwiches: B10CH11³⁻, B9CPH10²⁻, B10SH10²⁻.
  • The planar C2B3 face of nido-C2B4H8 (Deck 7) is a Cp-like ligand too; its complexes (Grimes) can be stacked into multidecker sandwiches.

Interchangeable fragments

Hawthorne's work showed that Fe(η-C5H5), Co(η-C2B9H11), BH and CH⁺ can replace one another in a cage: the first statement of what became the isolobal principle for clusters.

Mingos (2021), p. 22; multidecker sandwiches: R. N. Grimes, Carboranes, 3rd edn, Academic Press, 2016 (recalled reference).
Mingos credits Hawthorne's group with this observation, which preceded the general isolobal formulation by Hoffmann.
Metallacarboranes · beyond twelve

Polyhedral expansion: past the icosahedron

  • Reduce a closo carborane by 2 e⁻: it opens to a nido dianion (n + 2 pairs).
  • Add a metal fragment to the open face: the cage closes again with one more vertex.
  • From C2B10H12 this gives 13-vertex metallacarboranes such as CpCo(C2B10H12), larger than any closo borane anion known at the time.
Method of G. B. Dunks, M. M. McKown, M. F. Hawthorne, J. Am. Chem. Soc. 1971, 93, 2541 (recalled reference); counting as in Das, §10.12.8 (closo + 2e → nido).
Reduction-metallation is Wade's rules used as a synthetic plan: add two electrons to open the cage, then add a vertex that brings no extra skeletal electrons beyond what is needed.
Metallacarboranes · applications

What metallacarboranes are used for

Radioactive waste

Chlorinated cobalt bis(dicarbollide) is the key extractant in the UNEX process (Idaho–Khlopin Institute). Countercurrent tests on real acidic tank waste removed 99.95 % 137Cs, 99.995 % 90Sr and 99.96 % of α emitters.

Catalysis

Rhodacarboranes such as (Ph3P)2RhH(C2B9H11) were studied by Hawthorne's group as hydrogenation and isomerisation catalysts.

Weakly coordinating anions

[Co(C2B9H11)2]⁻ ("COSAN") is large, robust and charge-diffuse: it extracts large cations and stabilises reactive ones.

Redox units

Fe, Co and Ni sandwiches change oxidation state reversibly, like ferrocene, and are used in redox-active materials.

R. S. Herbst et al., Sep. Sci. Technol. 2003, 38, 2685 (UNEX); catalysis and COSAN: Greenwood & Earnshaw, 2nd edn, §6.8; Grimes, Carboranes (recalled).
The UNEX figures are from the 2003 paper (INEEL tank waste). The other three cards summarise well-known uses; check details before examining them.
Did you know?

A carborane sandwich that pulls caesium out of nuclear waste

  • Caesium-137 and strontium-90 are the main heat-producing fission products in reprocessing waste.
  • The cobalt bis(dicarbollide) anion is large, hydrophobic and very weakly coordinating, so it carries Cs⁺ into an organic solvent and survives strong acid and radiation.
  • Developed first in Czech and Russian laboratories and then jointly with the USA, it is the "universal" extractant of UNEX.

Summary

Key points

  • Boranes bind metals through M–H–B 3c–2e bridges (BH4⁻, B3H8⁻) or by a metal fragment becoming a cage vertex.
  • Isolobal vertices: BH ≈ Fe(CO)3 ≈ CpCo (2 skeletal e); CH ≈ Co(CO)3 (3 e). Wade's rules then classify the cage.
  • [C2B9H11]²⁻: five face orbitals, six electrons, η5 like Cp⁻; [Fe(C2B9H11)2]²⁻ is the carborane ferrocene.
  • 18-electron and n + 1 (or mno) counts agree; exceeding 18 e gives slipped sandwiches.
  • Reduction then metallation expands cages beyond 12 vertices; cobalt bis(dicarbollide) is used to extract Cs and Sr from waste.
Hawthorne (1967), §5.7; Das, §10.12.11 and §10.13.3; Mingos (2021).
Ask students to count one metallaborane by both the isolobal replacement and the direct v + x − 12 method.

Questions (press → to reveal each answer)

1. Which fragment can replace BH in B5H9: Fe(CO)3, Co(CO)3 or Fe(CO)4?
Fe(CO)3 (8 + 6 − 12 = 2 e, three orbitals). Co(CO)3 gives 3 e (like CH); Fe(CO)4 uses only two orbitals.
2. Why does C2B9H11²⁻ resemble Cp⁻?
Its open pentagonal face offers five orbitals with six electrons, the same as the π system of C5H5⁻.
3. Count [Co(C2B9H11)2]⁻ by the 18-electron rule.
Co³⁺ d⁶ + 2 × 6 = 18 e.
4. Classify (C4H4)Fe(CO)3 as a cluster.
4 CH (12) + Fe(CO)3 (2) = 14 e = 7 pairs, n = 5: nido square pyramid, isolobal with B5H9.
